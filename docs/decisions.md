# Decisions

A decision recorded here is settled. To change one, add a new entry that
supersedes it and update the affected documents in the same change.

"Owner" means the project owner chose it. "Design" means it was chosen during
design and can be challenged with evidence.

| # | Decision | By | Why | Consequence |
|---|----------|----|-----|-------------|
| D-001 | The engine is written in Rust. | Owner | Performance, control over memory layout, and a compiler that catches most cross-module mistakes in a large codebase written by many agents. | Slower builds; no garbage collector to lean on. |
| D-002 | The primary frontend is a glyph grid in its own window. A terminal frontend exists with reduced effects. | Owner | Cutscenes need shake, zoom, slow motion and sub-cell motion. | The simulation outputs an abstract glyph scene; frontends are thin. |
| D-003 | Time is one shared timeline. Actions have phases with durations. The game pauses for decisions. | Owner | Speed, reactions, interrupts and clashes come from one mechanism. | No "turns" anywhere in engine code. |
| D-004 | The power ceiling is planet-buster. Nearby destruction is per cell; distant destruction is coarse and realised on arrival. | Owner | The fiction demands it, and the coarse layer is needed for off-screen events anyway. | Stamps and devastation (02 §17). Magnitudes span about 33 orders. |
| D-005 | The world away from the player runs as a layered, abstract simulation. | Owner | A world that moves without the player, at bounded cost. | Hot, warm and cold levels; promotion and demotion of characters. |
| D-006 | One z-level is one storey of 3 m. Moving things also carry an exact height. | Owner | Maps stay authorable and readable; motion stays smooth. | Cells are 1 × 1 × 3 m. Collapse and craters are storey-grained. |
| D-007 | Bodies are anatomical and data-defined. | Owner | Injuries, severing and executions are simulated outcomes. | Tissue shares the material table and damage rule with terrain. |
| D-008 | Modding is JSON and EOC first. A scripting hook is reserved; nothing depends on it. | Owner | Deterministic, safe, reloadable, and familiar from CDDA. | The EOC verb list must be broad enough to hold all of the bundled content's rules. |
| D-009 | The simulation is deterministic. | Design | Replays, golden tests, reproducible bugs, cutscenes that cannot disagree with outcomes, a path to lockstep co-op. | The rules in 01 §4, enforced by lints and tests. |
| D-010 | A tick is one microsecond, stored as `i64`. | Design | Fine enough for characters thousands of times faster than human; range of 292,000 years. | Durations in data are converted at load. |
| D-011 | Scheduled work belongs to clocks arranged in a tree, each with a rate. | Design | Time stop, slow fields and time-dilated realms become one feature. | The timeline must reschedule on rate change (F3). |
| D-012 | Space and time are integers. Physical magnitudes are `Real`, a deterministic `f64`, in SI units. | Design | Exact where exactness matters; wide range where it is needed; numbers a modder can look up. | No std transcendental functions; `libm` only. |
| D-013 | Chunk 32 × 32 × 8 cells; tract 32 × 32 column; region 64 × 64 tracts. | Design | Powers of two; tract footprint equals chunk footprint; a cell index fits in 13 bits. | Constants live in `dims` and nowhere else. |
| D-014 | A cell has three element layers (floor, block, fixture). An element's state is definition + material + bits, stored in per-chunk palettes. | Design | Independent destruction of floor and wall; any shape in any material; compact storage. | Natural ground has no floor layer; standing uses the solid top below. |
| D-015 | One material table serves terrain, items and bodies. | Design | One damage rule everywhere. | Material definitions carry properties for all three uses. |
| D-016 | Cells change only through `WorldEdit`, which records cause and emits events. | Design | Caches, saves, events and attribution cannot drift. | Chunk mutators are crate-private. |
| D-017 | `clash-world` never calls into the simulation or EOC. It emits events. | Design | The world can be built, tested and reasoned about alone. | No veto hooks from data in the world; use flags and definitions. |
| D-018 | Tracts are loaded on demand by interest tickets. There is no fixed bubble. | Design | Fights range over kilometres; processes must be able to finish. | A loaded-tract budget with force-resolve as the pressure valve. |
| D-019 | Changed tracts are stored in full. Unchanged tracts are regenerated. | Design | Robust to generator changes where it matters; small saves. | Pristine tracts follow mod updates. Configurable. |
| D-020 | Damage is a packet of energy and intensity against a threshold and a capacity. | Design | Distinguishes many weak hits from one strong one; conserves energy through obstacles; scales without special cases. | Callers must supply intensity. |
| D-021 | Blast intensity is released energy over swept volume, `E / (4/3·π·r³)`. | Design | Matches cube-root scaling of real explosions from grenades to impacts with one formula. | Thresholds for pressure waves are in pascals. |
| D-022 | Structural support is span propagation per storey, bottom up. No stress analysis. | Design | Cheap, deterministic, good enough to make collapse emergent. | No load limits initially (02 §26). |
| D-023 | Bodies in flight are point masses with a cross-section. No rotation. | Design | Legible, fast, deterministic. | Tumbling is presentation only. |
| D-024 | A stamp is stored once at the coarsest level containing its footprint and applied lazily at load. | Design | Planet-scale events cost one record. | Tracts track a stamp watermark. |
| D-025 | Subgrids are the single abstraction for moving pieces of world. | Design | Vehicles, ships, elevators and torn-off buildings share one mechanism. | Every position is a `Location` with a `GridId` from the start. |
| D-026 | Ambient conditions vary by altitude within a realm. | Design | Ground to orbit without changing realm. | Zero gravity in orbit is by declaration, not orbital mechanics. |
| D-027 | Planes are interaction masks on occupants. | Design | Astral and mirror layers without duplicating space. | Terrain exists on all planes. |
| D-028 | All bundled content is mods. Engine code names no content. | Design | Everything is replaceable; the modding surface is proven by use. | Fills, rubble and placeholders are named in data. |
| D-029 | Definition ids are namespaced `mod:name`. | Design | Mods cannot collide by accident; overrides are explicit. | Reference resolution rules in 01 §7. |
| D-030 | Entities use a purpose-built component store, not a third-party ECS. | Design, provisional | Deterministic order, controlled serialisation, reflective access, API stability. | Revisit in document 06 with a prototype. |
| D-031 | Tract storage is behind a trait; the default backend is `redb`. | Design, provisional | Atomic, incremental saves without designing a file format. | Replaceable if measurements say so. |
| D-032 | Documents are the source of truth. Code that must differ changes the document in the same change set. | Design | Many agents, no shared memory. | Reviews check documents and code together. |
| D-033 | CLASH2D is a genre-neutral engine kit: an ASCII immersive sim and superpower sandbox. Bundled content is small reference mods, not a flagship game. | Owner | The engine provides tools; creators provide results. | Rule R10. Supersedes the "first content mod is a modern city" note. Reference mods span several genres. |
| D-034 | The engine enforces no fairness. Nothing is scaled, softened or levelled. Authors balance their own content. | Owner | Ordinary people should be ordinary, and the gap to everything else should be felt. | No difficulty scaling, level scaling or damage caps in engine code. |
| D-035 | When a reaction window opens, and whether the player is asked, is decided by data-defined rules. | Owner | Different content wants different rhythms. | Document 03 defines the rule hooks and player-adjustable defaults. |
| D-036 | Zero, one or several controlled characters. Party control is optional and enabled per scenario. | Owner | Party play should be possible without being imposed. | `advance` returns decision requests that name the entity. |
| D-037 | Every action resolves from defaults and can also be fully specified. | Owner | Quick play and granular play are both wanted. | Document 07 must define defaults for every choice it exposes. |
| D-038 | Progression schemes are data. The engine supplies attributes, pools, counters and events. | Owner | Use-based skills, training, point-buy, levels and cheat skills should all be buildable and mixable. | No built-in experience or level system. |
| D-039 | All UI is built from a data-defined glyph toolkit, including the engine's own screens and main menu. | Owner | Megamods and total conversions need their own menus and screens. | Document 12 specifies the toolkit; bundled screens ship as a mod. |
| D-040 | Hitting many weak foes at once is an ability defined in data, not an engine shortcut. | Owner | Keeps one ruleset. | No "mook" concept in the engine. |
| D-041 | The world records truth; characters act on beliefs formed by perception. | Design | Stealth, disguise, deception, framing and manipulation need the two to differ. | Rule R11. AI may not read ground truth it has not perceived. |
| D-042 | Minds and bodies are separate and joined by a relation. Entities can contain entities. Instances are editable at runtime. | Design | Possession, raised corpses, soul transfer, mounts, hiding places and body-reshaping abilities become rearrangements of data. | Fixed in 01 §6 for document 06. |
| D-043 | Interactive props are ordinary elements that promote to device entities on demand. | Design | A world full of usable things must cost little until used. | 02 §5.5; document 16. |
| D-044 | World size and shape are per-realm data. There is no default planet. | Design | The owner had no preference, and the engine should not have one either. | Reference mods use small realms. |
| D-045 | Actions occupy channels. An entity may run several actions at once if their channel slots do not overlap. | Design | Walking while shooting, sustaining while fighting, and losing options with a lost limb all follow from one rule. | Bodies supply slots (document 06). |
| D-046 | There are two ways to be fast: tempo divides an action's phase times; a personal clock rate speeds everything about an entity. | Design | Skill and superhuman speed are different things and content should choose. | Both exist from the first build (03 §3.5). |
| D-047 | A reaction requires noticing. A window is noticed after a data-defined delay on the candidate's own clock, or not at all. | Design | Makes "too fast to react to" a fact of arithmetic. Implements D-034 in the timing system. | The engine does nothing by default when a window is noticed. |
| D-048 | Prompts, automatic reactions and plan interruptions are ordinary rules on window and perception events. | Design | Implements D-035 without a separate mechanism. | Bundled content ships the default rules. |
| D-049 | Entities hold plans. A decision is requested when a foreground action ends with an empty plan, or a rule interrupts. | Design | Long activities without constant prompting; AI and players share one structure. | 03 §5. |
| D-050 | A joint action is one action instance with several participants. | Design | Takedowns, grapples, carrying, mounting and group rituals need participants locked to one timeline. | 03 §8. |
| D-051 | Inheritance, patching and overriding operate on JSON trees before typed loading. | Design | One generic implementation for every definition type; exact source positions survive merging. | Typed loading uses a deserialiser over the spanned tree. |
| D-052 | Bare references resolve in the mod's own namespace, then its imports. Supersedes the "then core" wording first written in 01 §7. | Design | No mod is special, so total conversions need no special case. | `imports` defaults to `requires`. |
| D-053 | Hot reload is an in-memory save, a definition swap and a load. | Design | Reuses the path that save transparency already tests; no reload-specific code in subsystems. | Reload cost is proportional to loaded state. |
| D-054 | A profile is a named mod list with a boot screen. The engine runs with zero mods; placeholders live in the reserved `engine` namespace. | Design | Megamods and total conversions start as their own game. | 04 §3.3, §3.5. |
| D-055 | Settings that rules can read are simulation state, changed only by command. | Design | Keeps replays exact. | Display preferences are separate (document 12). |
| D-056 | Expressions are strings in a small infix language, compiled at load. Conditions may also be written as structures. | Design | Readable arithmetic; errors at load with positions. | Crate `clash-expr`. |
| D-057 | An effect is an object with one verb key. A rule with parameters can be used as a verb. | Design | Regular, schema-friendly, and lets mods build their own vocabulary. | Engine verbs take precedence over rule ids. |
| D-058 | Events are notifications (queued, after the fact) or hooks (immediate, alterable, cancellable). Hooks exist only for simulation-layer operations. | Design | Lets content reach into outcomes while keeping the world crate free of calls upward (D-017). | 05 §8.2. |
| D-059 | Rules are global or attached to an owner. Attached rules run only for events their owner takes part in. Recurring rules run on the owner's clock with a declared policy while unloaded. | Design | Thousands of rules cost nothing until relevant. | 05 §9. |
| D-060 | Variables are declared, typed and defaulted. Undeclared use is a warning, or an error under `strict_vars`. | Design | Misspelt variable names are the commonest bug in systems of this kind. | 05 §6. |
| D-061 | Every event, verb, property and function is registered with types and documentation; schemas and the modding reference are generated from the registries. | Design | The reference cannot drift from the engine. | Undocumented registrations fail tests. |
| D-062 | The kernel entity (place, tags, variables, control, clock, channels, plan, attachments) is fixed in 03 and extended by document 06. | Design | Lets time, rules and actions be built before the full entity model. | Document 06 may add, not remove. |
| D-063 | New dependencies: `schemars` for schema generation; `notify` for file watching in tools only. | Design | Standard, well-maintained crates for solved problems. | `notify` must never be a dependency of a simulation crate. |
| D-064 | Component types are a fixed engine set. Mods extend entities through variables, attributes, traits and rules. | Design | Full tooling, saving and type checking for everything content adds. | 06 §3. |
| D-065 | Three numeric primitives: attributes (base plus modifiers), pools (bounded, with rates), tracks (accumulating, with levels). | Design | Implements D-038: every progression scheme is built from them. | Pools and stocks are analytic between changes; nothing runs per tick. |
| D-066 | One concept, the trait, covers statuses, mutations, perks, blessings and curses. | Design | Fewer concepts; a status is a trait with a duration. | 06 §5. |
| D-067 | Bodies are trees of parts built of material layers. Hits resolve through the layers by the world's packet rule. There are no hit points. | Design | Implements D-007 and D-034 with the rule that already breaks walls. | Armour is worn layers. Toughness is material. |
| D-068 | Body functions, and stopping (`die`), are mechanisms. When a body stops is content. | Design | Undead, constructs and things that refuse to die need no special cases. | Bundled content supplies the usual rule. |
| D-069 | A mind is a component that can move between entities. A driver acts for a body without moving a mind. | Design | Implements D-042. | 06 §8. |
| D-070 | Characters are built from templates with choices. "Species" is a kind of template. Choices range from picking options to free part-by-part assembly. | Owner default | The owner asked for great expressive room; no preference between authored and assembled was given, so both. | Creation screens are driven by template data. |
| D-071 | Individuals are anonymous and regenerated until something makes them notable, after which they are stored. | Design | Implements D-005 at the level of people. | 06 §12. |
| D-072 | Perception is signals reaching senses. Detection is an awareness level evaluated on events, never every tick. | Design | Stealth follows from numbers; cost follows activity. | 15 §3–§5. |
| D-073 | Tracks, memories and relations refer to personas, not entities. | Design | Implements D-041: disguise, secret identities and framing need no special cases. | 15 §8. |
| D-074 | Static geometry is common knowledge to AI. Dynamic facts are never free. | Design | Full map memory for every character costs too much for what it adds. | Elements tagged `hidden` are excluded until perceived. |
| D-075 | There is no dialogue-tree system. Speech is messages and lines chosen by rules; talking raises a hook that rules fill with options. | Owner | Emergent reactions over authored trees. | A mod can still build trees from `ask`. |
| D-076 | Presentation keeps an event-sourced view model, plays events back over real time, and shows only what the party perceived. | Design | Needed for playback and cutscenes; implements R11 for the player. | 12 §5, §7. |
| D-077 | A frame is a glyph grid plus free sprites. Nothing essential may depend on what a terminal cannot draw. | Design | Implements D-002. | The log is complete. |
| D-078 | Cutscene triggers are evaluated in the simulation and emit cues. Choreography cannot alter, drop or reorder what happened. | Design | Triggers need simulation state at the moment of the event; outcomes must not depend on presentation. | 12 §8. |
| D-079 | Controls are content. Any action can be bound twice: quick, from defaults, and precise, asking for every parameter. | Design | Implements D-037 at the keyboard. | The engine has no built-in move or attack key. |
| D-080 | Ordinary screens see non-party entities only as perceived. Debug screens see truth. | Design | The interface must not leak what the character does not know. | Enforced at load by binding context. |
| D-081 | The window frontend supports the mouse. Everything remains reachable by keyboard. | Owner default | No preference given. | The terminal frontend is keyboard-first. |
| D-082 | Three vehicle forms: mount (a creature), craft (a device entity), vessel (a subgrid with a hull entity). | Design | Each maps onto something that already exists. | 11 §3. |
| D-083 | Vehicle performance is derived from parts, mass and medium. One resource bus per vehicle by default. | Design | Damage and load degrade vehicles without special rules. | Finer wiring in document 16. |
| D-084 | Every cell has a medium: gas, liquid or vacuum, with density and pressure. The sea is still liquid; waves are not simulated. | Design | Buoyancy, drag, depth pressure, underwater light and sound from one query. | 11 §4. |
| D-085 | Sealed volumes hold compartments that exchange gas and liquid through openings by pressure difference. Hulls fail under pressure by the ordinary damage rule. | Design | Flooding, air pockets, ballast, decompression and crush depth from one mechanism, for sea and space alike. | 11 §10. |
| D-086 | Vehicles move in any direction. A vessel's grid swings in quarter-turns. | Design | Exact interiors without shear. Settles 02 §26 item 3. | A ship heading diagonally is drawn at the nearest quarter. |
| D-087 | Travel bands are data. | Design | Sea, undersea, underground and whatever else content needs. | Supersedes the fixed list first written in 02 §20.1. |
| D-088 | New dependencies for the window frontend: `winit`, `wgpu`, `fontdue`. | Design | Standard choices. | Confined to `clash-front-window`. |
| D-089 | Survival, base building and automation are first-class uses of the engine, alongside gadgets and powered suits. | Owner | Inspirations: CDDA bases, RimWorld, Minecraft tech and magic mods; a superpower setting needs its equipment. | Document 16; scenes S24 and S25. |
| D-090 | Resources are data, each with a quantity and a rate. One network evaluator balances them for world grids, vehicles, suits and gadgets alike. | Design | Custom grids of any kind; one implementation to test. Supersedes the flow and stock kinds first written in 11 §6.2. | 16 §5. |
| D-091 | A network balances rates, sheds consumers by priority, and treats stores as straight lines between changes. | Design | Devices need real supply; nothing runs per tick. | Shortage propagates through converters. |
| D-092 | Conduits are a fourth, sparse cell layer hosted by floors and blocks. | Design | Wiring must not occupy the fixture or block of a cell. | 02 §5.1, §6.2 amended. |
| D-093 | Elements may be passive loads counted by their network without becoming entities. City-scale utilities are tract services, not wiring. | Design | A lit building or a powered city must cost almost nothing. | 16 §4.3, §4.4. |
| D-094 | Signals are values on networks. Logic is built from devices with expressions; the engine has no gate primitives. | Design | Mechanism, not meaning. | Bundled content supplies a basic set. |
| D-095 | Crafting, machine processing, growing and construction are one definition type: the recipe. Work is done at a rate and completion is a single scheduled event. | Design | One concept for modders; no per-tick machines. | 16 §8, §10. |
| D-096 | Unfinished work is a job with stored progress that anyone may continue. The job list is the interface to the AI layer. | Design | Base building by player and helpers; nothing lost to interruption. | Document 09 assigns work. |
| D-097 | An assembly is a device with module slots sharing a bus. Gadgets, powered suits, implants and vehicles are assemblies. What modules confer lasts only while they are supplied and intact. | Design | One mechanism for all equipment. | 16 §11; 11 §6 aligned. |
| D-098 | A sealed suit is a one-compartment sealed volume around its wearer. | Design | Life support, breaches and crushing reuse the hull rules. | 16 §11.4; 11 §10. |
| D-099 | Machines left behind catch up by running their own event logic on a private timeline with batching, or stay loaded by holding a ticket. | Design | Correct off-screen production without simulating the place. | 16 §13. |
| D-100 | Every interaction is a definition whose effects are rules. The engine has no built-in "open" or "use". | Design | Mechanism, not meaning. | 16 §3. |
| D-101 | Combat adds no timing system and no damage system. An attack is a block on an action and ends in a hit on a body or a packet on an element. | Design | One ruleset; everything in 03, 06 and 02 applies to fighting unchanged. | 07 §1, §3. |
| D-102 | Chance enters combat only through data-defined contests and shot dispersion. | Design | Content chooses how random fighting is, down to not at all. | 07 §4.1. |
| D-103 | A strike's intensity is its energy over the striking surface's area times its give. Surfaces also carry a span, which decides severing. | Design | Edge, point and fist differ for physical reasons; the same form as body impacts. | 07 §3.2, §6.1; 06 amended. |
| D-104 | A defence either avoids or interposes. What it can turn aside is capped by what the defender can hold. Nothing defends automatically. | Design | Implements D-034 in defence: skill does not stop a blow far beyond one's strength. | 07 §5. |
| D-105 | Energy a strike delivers that the target neither absorbs nor passes returns into the striking surface. | Design | Strength without durability harms its user; weapons wear on hard targets. | 07 §6.3. |
| D-106 | An authored share of a strike's energy pushes the target. Speed follows from mass; below thresholds nothing or a stagger, above them flight as a world body. | Design | Knockback at every scale from one formula. | 07 §7. |
| D-107 | Strikes aimed at each other and due within a window clash and are resolved once: near-equal powers lock and spill energy into the world, otherwise the stronger carries through at the difference. | Design | The engine's namesake. Scene S5. | 07 §8. |
| D-108 | A multi-target attack carries the energy that passed each target on to the next. | Design | Implements D-040: cutting down a rank is a consequence of energy and edge, and stops at whoever can stop it. | 07 §6.4. |
| D-109 | Takedowns and executions are joint actions with a forced role and strikes that cannot miss, still delivered through the body model. | Design | Choreographed and certain to connect, but not certain to kill. | 07 §10. |
| D-110 | Projectiles are bodies in flight with a striking surface. Instant shots by ray serve beams and reduced detail. | Design | Cover, penetration, arc and time of flight from the world's rules; fast characters can respond, others cannot. | 07 §11. |
| D-111 | Every strike takes an effort. Previews are pure and use only what the viewer could know. | Design | Holding back, informed precise targeting, and AI that can be surprised. Implements D-037 and R11 in combat. | 07 §3.3, §13. |
| D-112 | Stances, styles, combinations, feints and counters are content built from traits, branching phases and reactions. | Design | Mechanism, not meaning. | 07 §12; traits gain exclusive groups. |
| D-113 | An ability is one definition type for skills, spells, powers and gifts alike: a bundle of grants, passive effects, modes, a rank and parameters. The engine does not distinguish kinds. | Design | Mechanism, not meaning. A sword style and a planet-cracking beam are the same structure. | 08 §3. |
| D-114 | Abilities are conferred by templates, traits, equipment or rules, and are one instance across sources. Innate and equipped abilities are the same thing. | Design | A suit's repulsors and a mutant's eye beams should not need different systems. | 08 §3.3. |
| D-115 | Ability parameters are modifiable values, like attributes. | Design | One definition serves every rank; items, moods and other abilities can strengthen or weaken a power. | 08 §4.2. |
| D-116 | Costs are taken from pools, stores or items when an action starts, in full or not at all. A cost may allow overdraw from another pool. | Design | Pushing past limits at a price is a common story and should be one field. | 08 §5.2. |
| D-117 | Sustained abilities are modes with an upkeep that end when their pool empties. Concentration, gesture and voice are channels. | Design | No per-tick upkeep; interruption and silencing need no special cases. | 08 §5.3, §5.4. |
| D-118 | Reactive expressions: pure expressions re-evaluated only when something they read changes. | Design | Conditions such as "while not under water" without polling. Shared with device outputs. | 05 §7.5. |
| D-119 | Abilities go dormant by their own condition and are suppressed from outside by tag. Either confers nothing until it clears. | Design | Anti-magic, dampeners, weaknesses and requirements from one mechanism. | 08 §6. |
| D-120 | Areas may be anchored to an entity and may override gravity inside them. Light sources may be negative. | Design | Auras, carried fields, time bubbles, gravity wells and darkness. | 02 §8.3 amended. |
| D-121 | What an ability leaves in the world is bound to it and ends with it, by removal, restoration or release. | Design | Curses, summons and conjured matter clean up after themselves, or do not, by declaration. | 08 §7.3. |
| D-122 | Snapshots of an entity or a bounded box of cells can be taken and restored. They are local, capped and owned by an ability. | Design | Recall, mending and turning back a room. Closes the rewinding question of 03 for local cases. | 08 §7.4. Whole-world return is left to scenarios. |
| D-123 | Truth reaches a character only by being recorded as knowledge: `appraise` stores true values in that character's track, and screens may show those. | Design | Revealing abilities without breaking R11. | 08 §7.5; 15 amended. |
| D-124 | The engine supplies no protection from one's own power. Durability, control and immunity are things content gives on purpose. | Design | Implements D-034 for abilities; recoil and unowned releases already ensure it. | 08 §10. |
| D-125 | A mind is a set of goals, each scored by an expression. The highest is pursued through methods whose steps become the character's plan. | Design | Data-defined, explainable, and it produces the same actions a player would issue. | 09 §3, §4. |
| D-126 | Goals, methods and tactics compile in a mind context that can read only the character's own state, its beliefs and perceptions, and static geometry. Anything else is a load error. | Design | Rule R11 enforced by the compiler instead of by convention. | 09 §3.4. |
| D-127 | Moment-to-moment choices are made by scoring every available action against a purpose. Harm is judged by preview; control, escape and aid by the ability's own value function. | Design | Characters use trickery and flight on the same footing as blows. | 09 §6.1. |
| D-128 | A character estimates threat from what it has seen or been told another can do, and its mind may act on being outmatched. | Design | Implements D-034 from the weaker side: ordinary people should know to run. | 09 §6.2. |
| D-129 | Navigation is hierarchical over tracts, per-chunk regions and cells, rebuilt lazily when geometry changes. Many movers to one place share a flow field. | Design | Paths must survive a destructible world and scale to formations. | 09 §5. |
| D-130 | A group may have a collective that decides for it and issues orders. Orders must be heard. Formations are data. | Design | Command, and its loss, from mechanisms that already exist. | 09 §8. |
| D-131 | Warm characters are advanced in bulk and exchange harm by expected values from previews. | Design | Scene S19 at affordable cost. | 09 §9.1. Believability to be tested. |
| D-132 | Unloaded notable characters are agents with routines and long goals, costing nothing between wake-ups. What a character was doing survives unloading as an aim. | Design | A world that moves without the player, and characters who do not forget what they were about. | 09 §10. |
| D-133 | An incident has a cast and stages. Each stage has a live form (goals for real characters) and an abstract form (effects on records). Exactly one applies, and an incident can change between them midway. | Design | Things happen for reasons whether or not anyone is watching, and can be walked in on. | 09 §11. |
| D-134 | Incidents occur by rate (scheduled, never polled) or because someone started one. | Design | Ambient events and purposeful ones share a definition. | 09 §11.2. |
| D-135 | Knowledge lives in minds, groups, places (local knowledge) and channels, and moves between them by rules with delay and loss. | Design | Witnesses out of crowds, wanted lists, news, and investigations from the gap between record and recollection. | 09 §12. |
| D-136 | Site stocks, shipments, services, weather and calendars are cold-layer mechanisms with no meaning assigned by the engine. | Design | Trade, blackouts and storms for mods that want them, at no cost to those that do not. | 09 §13, §14. |
| D-137 | Every choice a character makes can be opened and read: goal scores, plan, options considered, route. | Design | A character that does something inexplicable is a content bug that must be findable. | 09 §16. |
| D-138 | Places are authored as maps: rows of characters per storey with a palette of cell specs. Maps take parameters, contain other maps, and turn and mirror. | Design | Familiar from CDDA, readable, diffable, and enough to draw anything on a storey grid. | 10 §3. |
| D-139 | Randomness in generation comes only from positional seeds, through a generation context for expressions. | Design | Keeps generation pure and independent of when or in what order it runs. | 10 §3.4. |
